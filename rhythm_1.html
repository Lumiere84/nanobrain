NANOBRAIN Rhythm LearnerSelf-tuning clocks, by Bruce Derrick “Kuumba” Williams

Nobody tells these clocks what to listen for. They tune themselves to whatever beat they hear, then keep that beat. Anything that breaks the rhythm stands out, like an insect landing in a spider’s web.

1Pick a rhythm

This is three weeks of made-up activity, such as traffic on a network. Each choice has a different beat, and one has none at all.

What happenedWhat NANOBRAIN predicted for week 3, without seeing it

2Listen and learn

36 clocks start at different speeds, from every 3 hours to every 10 days. As they listen, each one speeds up or slows down until it matches a beat in the data. Clocks that find nothing fade out.

3Watch the web

Now NANOBRAIN watches two new weeks, minute by minute. Add an intruder at any moment and see who notices: NANOBRAIN, which asks “is this out of rhythm?”, or a simple alarm, which only asks “is this too high or too low?”

4Proof test

One intruder at a time proves little. This hides 120 intruders at random times in fresh data, runs two clean weeks to count false alarms, and scores both detectors.

What is real here

The clocks are real adaptive oscillators, a published method (Righetti, Buchli and Ijspeert, 2006). Each clock changes its own speed in response to what it hears, so the beats are discovered, not programmed. You can see it on the “Odd machine cycle,” which repeats every 10.5 hours, a beat no calendar would guess.

Following the NANOBRAIN plan, the clocks are frozen once they lock on, and only the readout is trained: how much each clock counts. The alarm level is set from week 3, which the rhythm never trained on, so it reflects honest mistakes rather than memorized ones.

The limits are real too. The data here is made up to test the method. NANOBRAIN can’t learn activity with no rhythm, and it reports that instead of guessing. It can be fooled by an intruder who follows the normal rhythm exactly, or by one who was already present while it learned. That is why, inside ARACHNODE, it would be one sense among several, next to the Ninth Eye.
